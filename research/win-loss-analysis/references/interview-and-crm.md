# Win-loss interviews and CRM fields

Use this when the user asks for an interview guide, a summary template, outreach wording or the fields to capture at deal close. Produce the guide in the reply, filled in for the user's situation where they gave it, with `<placeholders>` for anything not given.

## Why interviews

Internal data says what happened. Buyer interviews say why. A rep's account is filtered; the buyer's is the real intelligence, and most teams skip it because it feels awkward.

## Who interviews

Not the rep: the buyer will sugarcoat to the person they just rejected or bought from. Use a product marketing person, a sales leader who was not on the deal, or an outside interviewer. Choose someone who asks real follow-ups and is not defensive.

## When

- Losses: within about 2 weeks of the decision.
- Wins: within about 30 days of close.
- No decision: within about 1 month; hardest to book, often the most revealing.

## Asking a reluctant buyer (draft only, until the user says to send)

Do not say "We'd like to understand why you didn't choose us" (it sounds like re-opening the deal). Say something like: "We're improving our process and would value 20 minutes of candid feedback. There is no sales agenda." Keep it to 20 to 30 minutes. A small thank-you (gift card or charity donation) signals respect for their time. If they decline a call, offer a short written survey.

## Question bank

Opening: "This isn't a sales call. We're trying to get better, and your honest feedback is the most useful thing you can give us."

Decision process:
- Walk me through how you made this decision. Who was involved, and what were the key criteria?
- When did you first realise which direction you were leaning? What triggered that?
- Were there internal disagreements? How were they resolved?

Buyer's journey:
- After our demo, what was the internal conversation like? Who was for it, who pushed back?
- Was there a moment the deal almost died internally?
- What did you share internally, with whom, and what questions came back?
- Did criteria come up internally that we never discussed?
- Who had the most influence on the final decision, and what mattered most to them?

Our performance:
- How well did our team understand your problem?
- Was there a moment you felt confident, or started to have doubts?
- If you could change one thing about how we engaged, what would it be?

Competitive (losses):
- What did the competitor do differently that resonated?
- Was there something they showed you that we did not?
- Was it mainly the product, the team, or something else?

Wins:
- What almost made you go a different direction?
- What would have made this easier or faster?
- Now you are using the product, does the decision feel right?

Churn:
- When did you first start thinking about leaving? What happened then?
- What did you try before deciding to leave?
- What are you using instead, and what does it do better for you?

## Interview rules

Do not argue with feedback, even if unfair; write it down. Do not try to re-open the deal. Do not ask leading questions ("our product was the best, right?"). Do not send a junior person.

## Summary template

Deal, outcome, interviewee role, date | Stated reason | Underlying reason | Decision criteria in order | Who decided | Competitor and what it did differently | Moments of confidence or doubt | Quotes (verbatim) | Follow-up actions.

## Fields to capture at deal close

Required: outcome (won, lost, no decision); loss category (required for losses); primary competitor; actual decision-maker title; champion; rep's reason (free text); buyer's reason (free text, from interview or email); deal source; number of stakeholders; sales cycle days; discount given (% off list); interview status (yes, no, scheduled, declined).

Optional: number of meetings; number of vendors evaluated; biggest objection and how it was handled; what the champion did internally; product gaps mentioned; reference or case study that resonated.

Getting reps to fill them in: use dropdowns for everything except the reason field; review the data in pipeline meetings; a team can make required fields a condition of closing the deal.

## Cadence

Match effort to volume. High volume and small deals: a monthly batch review, with deep-dives only for surprising losses. Medium: a deep-dive on every larger deal and a monthly batch. Low volume and large deals: a deep-dive on every deal and a quarterly trend review. At minimum, interview on every competitive loss. Once a year, run the full batch analysis on the prior 12 months and share it with product, marketing and leadership.
