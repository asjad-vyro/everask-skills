# Review rubric

## Criteria (rate each 1 to 5)

| Criterion | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Soundness | Is the approach technically correct? Any logical flaws, unstated assumptions, leakage between train and test? |
| Novelty | What is genuinely new versus an incremental change to known work? |
| Reproducibility | Are details sufficient to repeat it? Code, data, hyperparameters, seeds available? |
| Experimental design | Are baselines fair and current? Are ablations adequate? Are datasets appropriate and the metrics the right ones? |
| Statistical rigor | Multiple runs, error bars, significance tests, sample sizes? Do the claimed gains exceed the noise? |
| Scalability | Does it scale? Are compute, memory and data costs discussed? |

Scale: 5 excellent, 4 good, 3 adequate with gaps, 2 weak, 1 serious problem. Use "not assessable" where the paper gives no basis.

## Contribution level

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Landmark | Changes the field: a new paradigm or widely applicable breakthrough |
| Significant | Clear improvement over the state of the art with solid evidence |
| Moderate | Useful, valid, incremental |
| Marginal | Small gains or narrow applicability |
| Below threshold | Fundamental flaws or insufficient evidence |

## Focus by paper type

| Type | Focus |
|---|---|
| Empirical | Experimental design, baselines, significance, ablations, reproducibility, whether conclusions exceed the evidence |
| Theoretical | Proof correctness, reasonableness of assumptions, tightness of bounds, link to practice |
| Survey | Coverage, taxonomy quality, recency, real synthesis beyond listing |
| Systems | Architecture choices, scalability evidence, real deployment, engineering contribution |
| Position | Coherence of the argument, evidence for claims, fair characterisation of opposing views |
| Clinical or social science | Study design, sampling and selection bias, confounders, effect sizes with intervals, preregistration, ethics approval |

## Pitfalls to avoid

- Reviewing the paper you wish had been written.
- Demanding unreasonably large extra experiments.
- Penalising the paper for not solving a different problem.
- Letting writing quality outweigh technical contribution.
- Treating a missing citation to your own work as a weakness.
- Giving a summary with no critical analysis.
- Vague criticism. Say which section, what is wrong, and what would fix it.

## Final check before sending

- All major claims were weighed against the evidence.
- 3 or more strengths and weaknesses, each with a location in the paper.
- Ratings table complete, each with a reason.
- Questions target real ambiguities, not rhetorical jabs.
- Verdict consistent with the listed strengths and weaknesses.
- Major issues separated from minor ones.
