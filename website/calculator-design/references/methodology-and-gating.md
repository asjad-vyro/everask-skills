# Methodology note and gating

## Methodology note template
Fill with the real design; leave nothing as a made-up figure.

- What this estimates: one sentence.
- Formulas: each formula on its own line with named variables.
- Inputs you provide: list, with units.
- Assumptions built in: for each, the value, why, and its source or the word "assumption".
- Benchmarks: name and link a source for each, or remove the benchmark.
- Not included: costs, benefits or situations the maths ignores.
- Last reviewed: <date>. Review again when pricing, product or benchmarks change.
- Questions about the method: <contact or "ask us for the spec">.

## What to give free, what to gate
Free: headline result, breakdown, chart, the methodology note.
Gate with an email: a formatted PDF the visitor can forward, an emailed copy with inputs, a deeper custom analysis.
Gate with an account: saved scenarios, side-by-side comparison over time, alerts when assumptions change.

Test for a fair gate: if the visitor already has the answer, would the extra thing still be worth an email? If the answer is "no, I just wanted the number", the gate is a trap.

## Tier plan format
| Tier | What the visitor gets | Gate | Why it is worth the ask |
Keep it to two or three rows. Tier 2 must be more than the same result in a PDF: add the full input list, the formulas applied, and a comparison with an alternative scenario.

## Failure patterns to avoid
- Result hidden behind the form (lead-trap).
- Opaque or hidden method; unsourced "industry average".
- Many inputs, few used; or result with no units ("14X").
- Defaults never updated; mobile layout broken; no one told the calculator exists.
- Judging success by form completions rather than by how many leads later become customers.
