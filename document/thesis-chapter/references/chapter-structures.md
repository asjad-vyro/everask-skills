# Standard chapter structures (used only when no guideline is given)

These are common conventions, not any one university's rules; fields and countries vary. Say in the reply that they were assumed. Translate the headings into the chapter's language. Drop or rename sections to fit what the user's material supports.

## Introduction
1. Background and context of the topic
2. Problem statement (what is not known or not solved)
3. Aim and objectives
4. Research questions or hypotheses (exactly as the user gave them)
5. Significance or rationale
6. Scope and delimitations
7. Structure of the thesis (one line per chapter)

## Literature review
1. Purpose and scope of the review (how sources were chosen, only if the user said)
2. Thematic sections, each grouping the user's sources around one idea
3. Synthesis: agreements, disagreements, gaps
4. Link to the research questions
5. Summary

## Methodology
1. Research design and approach, and why it fits the questions
2. Participants or data sources, and sampling
3. Instruments or materials
4. Procedure or data collection
5. Data analysis
6. Validity, reliability or trustworthiness
7. Ethical considerations
8. Limitations of the method

## Results (Findings)
1. Overview of the data and how it is organised
2. One subsection per research question or hypothesis, in the user's order
3. Tables and figures with captions
4. Summary of the main results (reporting, not interpreting)

## Discussion
1. Summary of the main findings
2. Interpretation of each finding against the research questions and the user's sources
3. Implications (theoretical, practical)
4. Limitations
5. Recommendations for future research

## Conclusion
1. Restatement of the aim
2. Answers to the research questions in brief
3. Contributions
4. Limitations and future work
5. Closing remark
