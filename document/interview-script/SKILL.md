# Customer Interview Script

A good result is a script the user can read from during the call. Every question asks about something the participant actually did; none hints at the answer the user hopes for or asks for a prediction. The minutes add up to the interview length, and the whole script is in your reply.

## Step 1: Pin down the brief

Read the message and any attached files first (personas, hypotheses, a product brief, earlier interview notes). From them, note:

- **Topic**: the product, problem area or situation the interviews are about.
- **Participants**: who they are and what qualifies them, for example "trial users who sent one invoice and nothing after".
- **Goal and decision**: what the user needs to learn and what the answer will decide.
- **Hunches**: assumptions the user wants tested.
- **Length**: the interview time in minutes.
- **Draft questions**: any the user, a co-founder or a manager wants to ask. Step 4 covers them.

Decide whatever is missing and list it under "Assumptions" in the reply. Defaults: 30 minutes; a video call, recorded with the participant's permission; participants who dealt with the problem in the last few months; a goal of learning how they handle it today and what they have tried.

Do not ask the user questions before writing the script. Use `clarify` only when the message names no product, topic or audience at all ("write me an interview script"): ask what the interviews are about and who the participants are, once, then carry on with the answer.

Use only facts the user gave. Do not invent details about their product, customers, numbers or earlier research.

## Step 2: Pick the moments to ask about, and split the time

People tell accurate stories about specific recent events and unreliable ones about habits or the future. Build the core questions around real moments:

| Participants | Moments to walk through |
|---|---|
| Trial, lapsed or cancelled users | what was going on when they signed up; the first time they used it; the next time the need came up and what they did instead |
| Current customers | what was going on when they started using it; the last time they used it for real work |
| People who may have the problem (no product yet) | the last time they dealt with the problem and what they did about it |
| Anyone else | the last time they did the thing the user wants to understand |

Split the interview length like this. For other lengths, scale the numbers so the four parts add up to the length exactly.

| Length | Opening | Warm-up | Core | Wrap-up | Main core questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 min | 2 | 3 | 12 | 3 | 4 |
| 30 min | 3 | 5 | 18 | 4 | 6 to 7 |
| 45 min | 3 | 5 | 32 | 5 | 10 |
| 60 min | 4 | 6 | 44 | 6 | 14 |

## Step 3: Draft the questions

Use the participant's everyday words, one question per line, under 25 words each.

**Opening** (spoken, first person, ready to read aloud): who the interviewer is in one line, using the company or product from the brief and leaving "[your name]" for the user; that the call is to learn and not to sell; that there are no right or wrong answers and that honest answers, including "it didn't work for me", are the most useful; permission to record; confirm the time available. Mention a thank-you or incentive only if the user gave one.

**Warm-up**: two or three easy questions about the participant's role, their work and how long they have done the relevant activity. Nothing about the product yet.

**Core questions**, in four blocks in this order, with about half the core time on block A. Give each main question one or two follow-ups written for this topic, not just "tell me more".

- **A. The last time.** Take a specific, recent moment from Step 2 and walk through it: "Walk me through the last time you [did the thing]. What happened?" Then who else was involved, what tools or methods they used, how long it took, what happened next, and what they were trying to get done.
- **B. What got in the way, and what they did about it.** "What was the hardest part?" "What did you do when that happened?" "What have you tried to fix it? What happened?"
- **C. What it cost and what they have already put in.** "How much time or money did it take last time?" "What have you paid for or set up to deal with it?" "What else have you looked at? What happened?" For business buyers: "Who else has a say, or pays?"
- **D. How they judged the result.** "Last time, how did you know it had worked, or hadn't?"

Mark the last one or two core questions "(if time)" so the interviewer knows what to drop when the call runs long.

**Probes** for any interesting moment:

- "Tell me more about that." Opens up any topic.
- "Why?" asked gently, two or three times. Gets to root causes.
- "Can you give me a specific example?" Moves from opinion to fact.
- "What happened next?" Follows the story.
- "How did you feel when that happened?" Shows how strong the feeling was.

**Wrap-up** (spoken): "Is there anything I didn't ask that you think is important?" "Who else should I talk to about this?" Thank them for their time. Share next steps only if the user gave any.

## Step 4: Check every question

Before you reply, test each question the participant will hear, follow-ups included (not the opening), against this table. Rewrite any that fail.

| Fails when it... | Instead of | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Asks them to predict, wish or judge: would you, how likely, if we built, what features would you want, "if you could wave a magic wand" | "Would you use an app that files your expense reports?" | "Tell me about the last expense report you filed. What did you do, step by step?" |
| Suggests the answer | "Don't you hate chasing receipts?" | "What did you do about the receipts last month?" |
| Pitches or asks for a reaction to the user's idea | "We're building a tool that does X. What do you think?" | Cut it. Ask what they do today. |
| Asks for a reason people can't give | "Why don't you use the app more?" | "Walk me through the last time you opened it. What happened next?" |
| Packs two questions in one | "What tools do you use and how often?" | Ask one, then the other. |
| Can be answered yes or no | "Is reporting a pain point?" | "What was the hardest part of the last report?" |
| Could be asked about any product | "How do you feel about your tools?" | Name the moment: "Think of the last time you [did the thing]..." |

Draft questions from the user: keep each one that passes, word for word, and rewrite each one that fails into a question about what the participant did. Never put a failing question in the script as written.

If the user pasted questions and asked only for a check, skip the script. Reply with a table (Your question | Verdict | Rewrite), where the verdict is "Keep" or the problem in a few words, then the corrected list in the user's order.

## Step 5: Reply with the script

Put the whole script in the reply, in this order, with the minutes in each heading and a time plan that adds up to the interview length:

````
# Interview script: <topic> (<length> min)

**Goal:** <one sentence>. **Decision it informs:** <one sentence>.
**Who to interview:** <participants>
**Time plan:** Opening <n> + Warm-up <n> + Core <n> + Wrap-up <n> = <length> min
**Assumptions:** <short bullets, only for what the user did not say>

## Opening (<n> min)
> <spoken text>

## Warm-up (<n> min)
1. <question>

## Core questions (<n> min)
**A. The last time**
1. <question>
   - Follow-up: <follow-up>
**B. ...** (then C and D)

## Wrap-up (<n> min)
1. <question>

## Probes for any moment
<the five probes from Step 3, each with its purpose>

## Note sheet
<the note sheet below, in a code block>

## Rules for the call
- Ask about their life, not your idea.
- Ask about the past, not the future: answers about what they would do tell you nothing.
- Talk less and listen more: aim for the participant to speak about 80% of the time.
- Never pitch.
- Strong feeling (frustration, relief, a long story) points to a real pain or delight: dig in.
- Compliments are noise: "That sounds cool!" tells you nothing. Steer back to what they did.

## Changed from your draft
| Your question | What was wrong | Use instead |
````

Include "Changed from your draft" only when the user gave draft questions.

The note sheet:

```
Participant: [name or ID]    Date:
Job they were trying to get done:
Situation: [who they are, what was going on]
The last time: [what happened, step by step]
Tools and workarounds:
Biggest pain: [their words]
Already tried or paid for: [time, money, other tools]
What good looks like to them: [their words]
Surprise:
Best quote: [exact words]
Follow-up:
```

If the user asks for a file (Word or PDF), load the built-in `docx` or `pdf` skill with `skill_view`, build it from the same script and publish it with `publish_artifact` (kind `document`). Still put the script in the reply.

## Hard rules

- The whole script is in the reply, not only in a file.
- No question asks the participant to predict, wish or judge, and none suggests its answer.
- Nothing pitches the user's product or idea or asks the participant to react to it.
- The minutes add up to the interview length.
- Only facts the user gave: no invented statistics, quotes, customers or studies. Anything assumed is listed under "Assumptions".
- Write in the language the user wrote in.
