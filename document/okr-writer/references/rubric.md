# OKR quality rubric and anti-patterns

## Rubric (rate each: pass, risk or fail, with a one-line reason; add a fix for every risk or fail)

- Strategic fit: clear link to a strategy, parent OKR or customer problem.
- Objective quality: specific, qualitative, helps the team make tradeoffs (not a slogan, a task, a bundle of metrics or a project name).
- KR outcome quality: measures a change in customer behaviour, a business result or operational health, not tasks, features or milestones.
- Measurement quality: baseline, target, deadline and evidence source present, or explicitly marked as missing.
- Product influence: the team can plausibly move the number.
- Focus: 1 to 3 objectives, 2 to 4 KRs each.
- Guardrails: quality, reliability or risk is covered for any KR that pushes growth, speed or volume.
- Alignment: parent, peer and dependency relationships are clear.
- Operating rhythm: cycle, check-in cadence and review point are stated.
- Integrity: no link to pay or individual ratings, no invented data.
- Disclosure: present when work is pre-committed (feature-team or mixed), left out when the team is empowered.

## Anti-patterns (flag, then reframe)

- Feature-delivery KR ("Launch X"): rewrite as the change X should cause; move X to Initiatives.
- Task-count KR ("Run 10 interviews", "Ship 5 releases"): rewrite as the learning or result, or label it an evidence-generation KR.
- Vanity metric (rises without customer or business value): propose a metric tied to value.
- Activity objective (describes work, not a change in the world): rewrite as the desired state.
- Metric-stuffed objective (KPIs glued together): move the numbers to KRs.
- Too many OKRs (over 3 objectives, over 4 KRs each): force a ranking and cut.
- Cascading theatre (parent KR copied down with no ownership logic): rewrite around what this team can move.
- Roadmap wrapper (OKRs just relist the roadmap): full rewrite; roadmap items become initiatives.
- Missing baseline: target means nothing; mark `baseline needed`.
- Missing evidence source: mark `source not identified`.
- Lagging-only (team owns revenue but no product behaviour): add a leading KR.
- No guardrail on a growth, speed or volume KR: add a counter-metric.
- Pay coupling (scores feed bonuses or ratings): refuse and explain that people will sandbag targets and hide bad news.
- Individual OKRs: default to team OKRs; warn about sandbagging and false precision.
- Unsupported benchmark (a universal target with no source): flag it; ask for a source or mark as assumption.
- Pre-product-market-fit over-precision: when learning is the real goal, write a learning OKR.

## Scoring notes

- The 0.6 to 0.7 "good stretch" score applies only to aspirational OKRs. Committed, compliance, safety, reliability and contractual KRs aim for 1.0.
- OKR scores are a learning signal for the team, never an input to pay or individual performance reviews.
