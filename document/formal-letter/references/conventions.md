# Letter conventions by language

Use the entry for the letter's language. These are common conventions, not rules of law. Where a country is named, adjust the date and address layout to it. If the user's own wording differs, follow the user.

Always keep names, figures and terms exactly as the user gave them. Write placeholders in the letter's language. Text in square brackets below is a placeholder, never a value to copy.

## English
- Salutation: "Dear Mr/Ms/Mx [Surname]," when the name is known; "Dear Sir or Madam," when it is not. US business letters often put a colon after the salutation instead of a comma.
- Closing: UK and much of the Commonwealth: "Yours sincerely," after a named salutation, "Yours faithfully," after "Dear Sir or Madam,". US: "Sincerely," in both cases.
- Date: UK day month year; US month day, year. Write the month as a word to avoid confusion.
- Subject line: "Re: ..." or "Subject: ...", short, with the reference number.
- Enclosures: "Enclosures: ..." (or "Enclosure: ..." for one).
- Formality: polite, plain, no contractions.

## Spanish (Spain and Latin America)
- Salutation: "Estimado Sr. [Apellido]:" or "Estimada Sra. [Apellido]:" when the name is known; "Estimados señores:" when it is not. Spain also uses the more traditional "Muy señor mío:" / "Muy señora mía:" / "Muy señores míos:". Always a colon after the salutation, never a comma (the comma is an English habit the RAE says to avoid); the body starts on a new line with a capital letter.
- Closing: "Atentamente," or "Le saluda atentamente,"; a longer form: "Quedo a la espera de su respuesta y le saludo atentamente."
- Address the recipient as "usted".
- Date: "[ciudad], [día] de [mes] de [año]", month in lower case; if not given, `[fecha]`.
- Subject line: "Asunto: ..." with "Ref.: ..." for a reference number.
- Enclosures: "Anexos: ...".
- Formality: high; "solicito", "le agradecería que", "en el plazo de".

## Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal)
- Salutation, Brazil: "Prezado Senhor [Sobrenome]," / "Prezada Senhora [Sobrenome]," or "Prezados Senhores," when the name is unknown.
- Salutation, Portugal: "Exmo. Senhor," / "Exma. Senhora," or "Exmos. Senhores," when the name is unknown. This is the usual formal opening in Portugal, not only for authorities.
- Closing: Brazil "Atenciosamente,"; Portugal "Com os melhores cumprimentos,".
- Date: "[cidade], [dia] de [mês] de [ano]", month in lower case.
- Subject line: "Assunto: ..."; "Ref.: ..." for a reference.
- Enclosures: "Anexos: ...".
- Formality: address the reader as "o senhor / a senhora" or in the third person, not "você".

## French
- Salutation: "Madame, Monsieur," when the name is unknown; "Madame," or "Monsieur," when it is known: do not add the surname ("Monsieur [Nom]," is not used in a formal letter). With a known title: "Madame la Directrice," / "Monsieur le Maire,". Do not use "Cher Monsieur" / "Chère Madame" in an official letter.
- Closing: a full sentence is customary, for example "Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées." Repeat the salutation words in it ("Madame, Monsieur", "Monsieur le Maire").
- Date: "[Ville], le [jour] [mois] [année]", month in lower case; the first of the month is written "1er".
- Subject line: "Objet : ..." with "Réf. : ...". Add the line "Lettre recommandée avec accusé de réception" only if the user says they send it that way. A space goes before the colon.
- Enclosures: "Pièces jointes : ..." (or "P. J. : ...").
- Layout: sender top left, recipient on the right below it, then place and date on the right.
- Formality: "vous", conditional for requests ("je vous serais reconnaissant(e) de bien vouloir ...").

## Arabic
- Write right-to-left. Mention to the user that the text is right-to-left if they will paste it into a layout that does not support it.
- Opening: "السيد/السيدة [الاسم] المحترم/المحترمة،" or "السادة [الجهة] المحترمين،", then "تحية طيبة وبعد،" before the body.
- Subject line: "الموضوع: ..." and "المرجع: ..." for a reference.
- Closing: "وتفضلوا بقبول فائق الاحترام والتقدير،" or "مع خالص الشكر والتقدير،", then the name.
- Enclosures: "المرفقات: ...".
- Date: write the date the user gives, in the calendar the user uses (Gregorian or Hijri); do not convert. Use the digits the user used.
- Formality: Modern Standard Arabic, polite and direct; no dialect.

## Korean
- Order: recipient, then "제목: ..." (subject), then greeting and body; the date and the writer's name come last.
- Recipient: "[기관명] 담당자님께" for an organisation's staff; "[이름] 님께" for a person, or "[이름] 귀하" on a recipient line. Use either a title or 귀하, not both ("[이름] 선생님 귀하" is excessive).
- Greeting: "안녕하십니까." then the body.
- Body: the formal polite style (-ㅂ니다 / -습니다). Requests often end with "부탁드립니다" or "요청드립니다".
- Closing: "감사합니다." then the date and the writer's name, often followed by "드림" or the more deferential "올림".
- Date: year, month, day ("[연]년 [월]월 [일]일"); use the user's date or a placeholder.
- Enclosures: on the line after the body, "붙임" (no colon) then the name of each attached document.
- Formality: high; avoid casual endings.

## Indonesian
- Header block: "Lampiran: ..." (omit if nothing is attached) and "Hal: ..." (also written "Perihal: ..."), the subject. "Nomor: ..." is a letter number the sender assigns: include it only if the user gave one, and never invent one.
- Recipient: "Yth. Bapak/Ibu [Nama]" ("Yth." stands for "Yang terhormat"). Write "Yth." alone, not "Kepada Yth.".
- Opening line: "Dengan hormat," then the body.
- Closing: "Demikian surat ini saya sampaikan. Atas perhatian dan kerja sama Bapak/Ibu, saya ucapkan terima kasih." then "Hormat saya," and the name.
- Date: "[Kota], [tanggal] [bulan] [tahun]", month written as a word.
- Formality: "Bapak/Ibu" for the recipient, "saya" for the writer; formal and polite.

## German
- Salutation: "Sehr geehrte Frau [Nachname]," or "Sehr geehrter Herr [Nachname]," when known; "Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren," when not. A comma follows and the first word of the body is lower case unless it is a noun or "Sie".
- Closing: "Mit freundlichen Grüßen" (no comma after it), then the signature and the printed name. Avoid the old-fashioned "Hochachtungsvoll".
- Switzerland: write "ss" for "ß" ("Mit freundlichen Grüssen").
- Date: "[Ort], [TT.MM.JJJJ]" or "[Ort], [Tag]. [Monat] [Jahr]"; use the user's date or `[Datum]`.
- Subject line: no word "Betreff", set in bold, no full stop at the end; name the matter and any number, for example "[Anliegen], Kundennummer [Nummer]".
- Enclosures: "Anlage" (one) or "Anlagen" (several), below the signature.
- Formality: "Sie"; requests such as "Ich bitte Sie, ... bis zum [Datum] ..." when the user gave the date.

## Other languages
Use the neutral formal register of that language, the standard formal salutation and closing you are sure of, and say in your assumptions that the conventions are general. When unsure of a convention, write a plain, polite form rather than guess.
