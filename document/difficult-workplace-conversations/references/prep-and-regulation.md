# Preparation and staying calm

## Facts versus story
| Fact (observable) | Story (interpretation, leave out) |
|---|---|
| The report arrived after the agreed date | "They don't care about deadlines" |
| I was interrupted twice in the meeting | "They don't respect my opinions" |
| No reply to my email for a week | "They're avoiding me" |
Test: would a video camera have recorded it? If not, it is a story.

## Own contribution
Ask: Did I set clear expectations? Give the resources and support needed? Notice early signs and say something? Model what I expect? This is ownership of what the user controls, not blame.

## Their view
How might they describe this? What pressure or constraint might they be under? What might their good intent have been? What do they care about that I can acknowledge? Label all of this as guesses.

## Three goals
Outcome (what specific change), relationship (how we relate after), identity (who I want to be in the room, such as calm and direct).
Weak goal to better goal: "Make them apologize" becomes "Understand what happened and agree how to prevent it". "Prove I'm right" becomes "Share my view and understand theirs".

## Timing and place
Private and neutral, no power imbalance, enough time (30 to 60 minutes), not when either person is rushed or right before something high-stakes, and not late on a Friday when there is no time to follow up.

## Performance conversations
Have two or three specific examples, the expectation they were measured against, the support already offered, what help can be offered, and what happens if nothing changes.

## Conflict conversations
Is it a pattern or one event? Has it been raised before, and what happened? What common ground exists? What would resolution look like for them?

## Raise and promotion requests
Market data (research it), own achievements since the last change, timing against budget cycles, acceptable non-salary alternatives, and the fallback if the answer is no.

## Calming steps
Before: if anxious, write the worst case, how you would cope, the likely case and the best case. If angry, write an unsent letter, move your body, talk to someone uninvolved, sleep on it.
Five minutes before: feel your feet on the floor and name five things you see; breathe in for 4, hold 4, out for 6, four times; remind yourself of your positive intent; let go of needing to win.
During: pause ("Let me think about that"), speak slower than feels natural, unclench your jaw and drop your shoulders, ask what a neutral observer would see.
Exit gracefully if too triggered: "I want to give this the attention it deserves and I'm finding it hard to think clearly. Can we continue [tomorrow / in an hour]?"
After: note what went well, what was hard and what to do next; do not act on anger for 24 hours.

## When to escalate
Safety risk, legal issues, harassment or discrimination, repeated conversations that have not worked, a power imbalance that prevents resolution, or a need for documentation. Involve HR or the right manager.

## Common mistakes
Scripting every word (prepare themes and an opening, not a monologue), building a case to win, waiting too long, and hinting instead of stating. Write a direct opening.
