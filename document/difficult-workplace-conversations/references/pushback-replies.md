# Opening lines, pushback replies and closing lines

Adapt these to the user's facts. Replace every bracket with the user's details.

## Opening formula
Purpose + intent + invitation.
- Collaborative: "I'd like to talk about [topic]. My goal is to understand your side and share mine so we can [outcome]. Is now a good time?"
- Direct: "We need to discuss [topic]. I want to be straightforward and I also want to hear your side."
- Curious: "I've noticed [observation] and I'd like to understand what's happening. Can you help me see it from your side?"
- Performance: "I wanted to check in about [area]. I've noticed [observation] and I'd like to understand what's happening so we can address it together."
- Peer conflict: "I'd like to clear the air about [incident]. I value working with you and want to sort this out before it affects our work."
- Feedback to a manager: "I have some feedback I'd like to share because I want us to work as well as possible together. Are you open to hearing it?"
- Request (raise, promotion): "I'd like to discuss [compensation / my path here]. I've prepared my thoughts and I'd like your view on where I stand."
Avoid: "We need to talk about your performance", "I've been meaning to tell you", anything starting with "You always".

## Sharing the point (SBI as an I-statement)
"In [situation], when [behavior], the impact was [effect on me / team / work]. I'd like to understand what happened on your end."

## Replies to pushback
Denial ("That didn't happen", "That's not what I said"):
- "Help me understand how you remember it, and let's compare notes."
- "Whether or not we agree on what happened, can we agree on the impact?"
Deflection ("What about when you...", "Everyone does that"):
- "I'm happy to talk about that separately. Right now I'd like to finish this one."
- "You may be right about that, and I still need to address this."
Attack or "You're overreacting":
- "I hear that you feel this is unfair. Tell me what fair looks like to you."
- "I'm trying to have a constructive conversation. What do you need from me?"
Defensiveness:
- "I'm not trying to attack you. I want to understand. What's your main concern right now?"
Shutting down (silence, one-word answers):
- "I notice you've gone quiet. What's coming up for you?"
- "Would it help to take a break and come back to this?"
Emotional (tears, raised voice):
- "I can see this matters to you. Take your time. We can pause."
Drifting off topic:
- "I want to make sure we cover the main issue. Coming back to [point]..."
Acknowledging without agreeing:
- "I understand that's your experience." / "That makes sense from your side. I still see the impact as [x]."
Buying time:
- "Let me think about that for a moment." / "Can we slow down? I want to make sure I understand."
If the user is triggered:
- "I'm noticing a strong reaction in myself. Give me a moment." / "Can we take five minutes and continue?"

## Reframes
- Blame to curiosity: "Why did you miss it?" becomes "Help me understand what happened with the timeline."
- Character to behavior: "You're unreliable" becomes "When deadlines are missed, it creates problems for the team."
- Past to future: "You always do this" becomes "Going forward, how can we handle this differently?"
- Position to interest: "I need you to do X" becomes "The outcome I'm after is Y. What would work for you?"

## Closing
- Summarize: "Let me check I have this right: [A] will [action] by [date], [B] will [action] by [date], and we'll check in on [date]. Does that match your understanding?"
- Follow-up: "I'd like to check in on this in [timeframe]. Does [day] work?"
- No resolution: "We don't seem to agree today. I'd like to [get more information / sleep on it / involve X]. Can we reconvene [when]?"
- Door open: "If anything comes up before then, tell me and we'll deal with it as it happens."

## 24-hour follow-up message (draft)
Subject: Following up on our conversation
"Hi [name], thank you for talking today. I appreciated your openness. Here is what I understood we agreed: [action 1], [action 2]. Let's check in on [date]. Tell me if anything comes up before then."

## Scenario openers
- Receiving feedback: "Thank you for telling me." then "Can you give me a specific example?" then "I need some time to think. Can I come back to you [when]?" then "I'm going to work on [action]."
- Escalating: "I've tried to address this directly and I need your help. Here's what happened: [facts]. Here's what I've tried: [attempts]. I'm hoping you can [ask]."
- Upward feedback: "When [situation], I find myself [struggling] because [impact]. I'm wondering whether [alternative] might work better. I may be missing context."
