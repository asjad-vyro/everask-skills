# Difficult Conversation Planner

A good result is a plan the user can read in five minutes and say out loud: facts not judgments, an opening that invites dialogue, scripted replies for the likely reactions, and a dated follow-up. The plan goes in the reply itself, in full.

## Step 1: Read the situation

Work from what the user wrote. Do not ask questions and do not open with a list of them. Pick out: who the other person is (report, peer, manager, other), what happened, what the user wants, and any emotion or history they mention. If something needed is missing (a date, the exact words said), write `[not given]` or state an assumption in one line. Never invent facts, dates, quotes, numbers or history the user did not give.

Classify the conversation as one of: performance or accountability, peer conflict, upward feedback or request (raise, promotion, workload), receiving or responding to feedback, sensitive topic.

If the situation involves harassment, discrimination, threats, safety, a possible dismissal or other legal exposure, tell the user at the top of the plan (2 sentences) to bring in HR or a legal adviser first, then still give the plan for the part that is theirs to handle. Add one plain line at the end: this is general communication guidance, not HR or legal advice.

## Step 2: Prepare the content

Read `references/prep-and-regulation.md` once, for the fact/story split, the goal types, the contribution check and the calming steps. Use it to do this thinking before you write:

1. Facts: what a video camera would have recorded. Move every interpretation ("doesn't care", "avoiding me") into a separate "story I might be telling myself" line.
2. Impact on the user, the team, the work. Use only impacts the user stated or that clearly follow; mark others as assumptions.
3. The other person's likely view and constraints, labelled as guesses.
4. One thing the user may have contributed (unclear expectation, late notice, no check-in). Phrase it as a question to the user's own conduct, not an accusation.
5. Three goals: the outcome, the relationship, and the tone the user wants to hold.

## Step 3: Write the scripts

Read `references/pushback-replies.md` once, for opening formulas, replies to denial, deflection, attack, shutdown and tears, and closing lines. Adapt the wording to the user's actual situation: use their details, not the template brackets. Every spoken line is written so the user could say it as it stands.

## Step 4: Deliver the plan

Reply with exactly these sections, in this order, using the user's details throughout:

```
# Conversation plan: <topic in a few words>

**With:** <role or name given> | **Type:** <type from Step 1> | **Assumptions:** <list, or "none">

## 1. Goal
Outcome: ... / Relationship: ... / How I want to show up: ...

## 2. Facts and impact
- Situation: <when and where, or [not given]>
- Behavior: <observable, no judgment>
- Impact: <effect on me, the team, the work>
- A story I should not assume: <interpretation to leave out>

## 3. Their likely view (guesses)
...

## 4. Opening line (say this first, about 30 seconds)
> <purpose> + <intent> + <invitation>

## 5. Key points
1. <situation, behavior, impact in an I-statement, no "always" or "never">
2. ...
3. <the specific ask or change>

## 6. Questions to ask them
- 3 open questions

## 7. If they push back
| They say or do | You say |
|---|---|
| Deny / "that did not happen" | "<line>" |
| Deflect / "what about when you..." | "<line>" |
| Get defensive or attack | "<line>" |
| Go quiet | "<line>" |
| Get emotional | "<line>" |
| <one reaction specific to this situation> | "<line>" |

## 8. Landing it
- Agreement to confirm aloud: who does what by when (use a real date only if the user gave one; otherwise "<within N days, suggested>")
- Check-in: suggested within 1 to 2 weeks, labelled as a suggestion
- Follow-up message within 24 hours (short draft, not sent)
- If it does not resolve: <the next step, such as pausing and reconvening, or involving a manager or HR>

## 9. Before you walk in
- Timing and place (private, enough time, not late on a Friday)
- 3 short calming steps, and one line to use if you get triggered
```

For a very short request, keep every section but make each one brief. Keep the whole plan to about one page of reading, with the pushback table at three to six rows.

## Step 5: Situation-specific rules

- **Raise or promotion request:** do a `web_search` (one or two calls) for current pay ranges for the role and location the user gave, and link what you found. If the user gave no role or location, say the research step is left to them. Never state a salary figure the user or a search did not supply. Build the case from the user's own achievements, and include a fallback ask (title, scope, review date, development budget) in case the first ask is refused.
- **Upward feedback:** tie the point to the manager's priorities and give one alternative the user proposes.
- **Peer taking credit or similar conflict:** the opening names the specific instance, not the pattern.
- **Receiving feedback:** replace sections 4 and 5 with how to acknowledge, clarify, ask for time and commit.
- **User is angry or hurt:** add one line to section 9 recommending a pause (a night if possible) before the conversation, and a draft they do not send.

## Hard rules

- Describe behavior and impact, never character. No "you always", "you never".
- No invented facts, quotes, dates, names or statistics. Use `[not given]` or a labelled assumption.
- Do not ask the user questions; decide, state the assumption, and deliver the full plan.
- Any message to send (a follow-up email, a calendar invite) stays a draft until the user says to send it.
- Do not pad with theory. The reply is the plan.
