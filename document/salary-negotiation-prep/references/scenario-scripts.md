# Scenario scripts

Word-for-word lines for the common moments in a negotiation. Words in capitals (ROLE, CITY, LOW, HIGH, OFFER, ASK, TARGET, AMOUNT, OTHER, REASON, ITEM, DAY) stand for the user's real facts and figures: replace every one before a line goes into the reply. Where the user gave no REASON, use a plain true one from what they said, or leave the line out. Keep the lines short and warm; the user may say them out loud.

## 1. The first offer is below the market range

Do not accept at once. Thank them, show enthusiasm, and counter with a sourced figure.

"Thank you, I'm excited about this role and the team. The base is lower than I expected: the range I found for ROLE in CITY runs from LOW to HIGH. I'd be glad to accept at ASK. Is there room to move toward that?"

## 2. They ask for your salary expectations first

First reply: "I'm flexible and focused on finding the right fit. What range have you budgeted for this role?"

If they press: "From what I've found for this role and market, I'm looking at LOW to HIGH, depending on the full package, and I'm open to talking through the whole picture."

Choose a range whose lower end is a figure the user would be glad to accept.

## 3. They say the base is fixed, or that this is the best they can do

"I understand the base is set, and I appreciate you telling me straight. Could we close the gap another way? A signing bonus of around AMOUNT, or a pay review at six months with a clear path to TARGET, would make this an easy yes for me."

Other things to ask for in place of base: more equity, extra vacation days, remote days, a training budget, a better title, a later start date, relocation help. A signing bonus is a one-time cost, which often makes it easier for a company to approve.

## 4. The user has another offer

Use this only if the offer is real and in writing. Do not name the other company unless the user wants to. Frame it as wanting to make this work, never as a threat.

"I want to be open with you: I have another written offer with a base of OTHER. REASON is why this is my first choice, and I'd like to make it work. Could you get to ASK?"

## 5. They ask what the user earns now

"I'd rather focus on the role and the value I'd bring than on my current pay, which isn't a like-for-like comparison because REASON. From my research I'm targeting LOW to HIGH. What range have you budgeted?"

Some US states and cities, and some other countries, stop employers from asking about pay history. The rules differ and change, so search for the user's location, link what you find and call it general information. If the user chooses to answer, the answer must be true.

## 6. They want an answer right away

"Thank you, I'm very keen on this and I want to give it the careful look it deserves. Could I come back to you by DAY? Could you also send the full offer in writing?"

If the deadline cannot move: "Understood. Tell me what's flexible on your side and I'll tell you where I can land."

## 7. They say yes, or come back with a new number

Yes: "That works for me, thank you. Could you send the updated offer letter with these terms so I can review it and sign?"

A new number between the walk-away point and TARGET: "Thank you for working on this. I'm close. If you can reach TARGET, or add ITEM, I can say yes." Use this only if the user would accept.

A new number below the walk-away point: "Thank you for trying. I'd like to find a way to join, but at this level I can't make it work. Is there anything else you can move?"
