# Product-Built Virality

Read this only when the product is used with other people, when its output reaches non-users, or when the user asks about viral loops, product-led growth or word of mouth. Virality can be built into the product instead of bought with rewards, but do not force it where it does not fit.

## Is the product a fit?

**Natural fit (build for it):**
- **Collaboration tools:** value grows when you invite others (docs, whiteboards, project management).
- **Communication tools:** you cannot use them alone (email, scheduling, messaging).
- **User-facing outputs:** every use produces something others see (designs, videos, forms, links).

**Limited fit (do not force it):**
- **Backend and infrastructure products:** invisible to end users.
- **Competitive-advantage tools:** users hide that they use them, because it is their edge.
- **Internal-only tools:** they never leave the organization.

On the limited end, put the effort into a reward program, content and partnerships instead of loops that will not fire.

## Six product-built mechanisms

Most of these need no reward: the loop is part of the product.

1. **"Powered by" badges.** A small attributed badge on user-facing output, so every page, form or widget a customer ships shows the product. Often free-tier only, with the paid tier removing it.
2. **Exposure loops.** Normal use shows the product to non-users. A scheduling link shows the tool to every recipient. An email signature such as "Sent via ..." works best when using the product confers status, so recipients want it.
3. **Social sharing.** Make output natively shareable with a branded hook: a hashtag such as "Made with ...", or one-tap sharing of a milestone, result or artifact to the social network the audience uses.
4. **Embeds.** Let users embed their content elsewhere, with the brand and a link back. Embedded docs, designs and videos carry the product to every viewer on every host site.
5. **Watermarks.** Like a badge but baked into the output and harder to remove. The free tier carries the mark and the paid tier removes it, so free users become the distribution.
6. **Product-driven word of mouth.** The purest form: the product is so good or useful that people tell others unprompted. It cannot be bolted on; it is earned through the product experience, and the other mechanisms make it easier to trigger.

A reward-based referral program is the seventh option and the only one paid for. Use it when the product does not spread on its own. It is covered in the main steps.

## How to use this in the answer

Pick one or two mechanisms that fit the product. For each, say exactly where it appears in the product, what a non-user sees, and what the product owner has to build. Do not list all six.
