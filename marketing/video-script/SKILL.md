# Video Script and Shot List

Write a script someone can record from, with every line paired with what the viewer sees.

A script fails in two common ways: it reads like an essay, so it sounds stiff out loud, or it describes only the words, so the edit has nothing to show. This skill fixes both by timing the spoken words and planning a picture for every line.

## Step 1: Pin down the brief

Use what the user gave: the message, attached files (a blog post, notes, a product page, an old script) and any links. Settle these points:

- **Topic and goal**: teach, sell, announce, or tell a story.
- **Audience**: who's watching and what they already know.
- **Platform and length**: if none is given, use 6 to 8 minutes for YouTube and 30 to 45 seconds for a Short, Reel or TikTok.
- **Who's on screen**: a presenter, a voiceover over visuals, an AI avatar, or no voice with text only.
- **The call to action**, if there is one.
- **Brand voice**, if they gave examples.

Ask a question only if you can't tell what the video is about. Otherwise state your assumptions in one line at the top of the script.

## Step 2: Get the facts right

Every figure, name, quote and claim comes from the user's material. If the topic needs facts the user didn't give, find them with web search and list the sources at the end. If you can't confirm a fact, write `[ADD: what goes here]` instead. Never invent statistics, studies, customers or quotes.

## Step 3: Pick the structure

- **Short (60 seconds or less)**: hook (0 to 2 seconds), then one idea, the payoff, and an optional call to action. One point only; cut everything else.
- **Explainer (1 to 5 minutes)**: hook, then tension (what people think compared with what's true), 2 to 5 ideas, the key insight, a proof or example, why it matters, and a close. Link sections with "but" or "so", never "and then". Reach the hook and the tension by 0:30.
- **Long YouTube video (8 minutes or more)**: a cold open that makes a promise, and the first payoff before 2:00. Chapters of 2 to 4 minutes, at most 5 or 6 for a 10 to 15 minute video. Re-hook about every 3 minutes ("the surprising part is still to come"). End with the call to action and a reason to watch the next video.
- **Tutorial**: show the finished result first, then the steps. Each step is one action and why it matters.
- **Product or promo**: the problem, then the product in action, the proof, the offer and the call to action.

## Step 4: Write three hooks

Write three different openings, then pick one and say why. The hook types are:

- a contrarian claim ("Everyone gets this wrong");
- the outcome ("By the end, you'll be able to...");
- a mystery or story ("In 2019, a bakery...");
- stakes ("This mistake costs you...");
- the result shown first.

For each hook, give what's seen, what's said and the on-screen text. The voice starts on the first frame, and the first frame shows something worth stopping for. Don't open with a logo, a greeting or "in this video".

## Step 5: Write the script

Write it as a table:

| Time | Voiceover or dialogue | On screen | Text on screen | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Time the words.** Narration runs at about 150 words a minute (2.5 a second), and casual speech about 170. Count each section's words, and keep the total within 10% of the target length.
- **Write for speaking.** Use short sentences and contractions, one idea per sentence. Read each line aloud in your head; rewrite anything you'd stumble over.
- **Plan a picture for every line.** Describe what the viewer actually sees ("close-up of the inbox counter dropping to zero"), not a mood ("satisfying moment"). Change the picture every 3 to 5 seconds in an explainer and every 1 to 3 seconds in a short.
- **Mark cutaways.** For each cutaway, say whether it's filmed by the user, stock footage (when it must look real: places, people, products), or generated (abstract ideas, diagrams, data, impossible shots).
- **Keep on-screen text short.** Use 3 to 5 words for key numbers, terms and steps. Shorts need captions throughout, because most people watch them muted.
- **Direct the voice.** Put the words to stress in **bold**. Add `[pause]` before a reversal and after a big claim, and one delivery note per section (pace and energy, not "be natural").
- **Cue the sound.** Give the music's mood and energy for each section, and where it drops out or comes back. Use sound effects sparingly, on transitions and reveals.

## Step 6: Check it

- The word count fits the length.
- Every fact traces to the user's material or a listed source.
- No stretch longer than 5 seconds goes without a new picture (an explainer) or 3 seconds (a short).
- Every `[ADD: ...]` placeholder is listed for the user.

## Step 7: Deliver

Reply with, in order:

1. a one-line header with the title, length, platform, audience and goal;
2. the three hooks, with your pick;
3. the script table;
4. the cutaway list, grouped into "film", "stock" and "generate";
5. the sources, and the placeholders still to fill.

For a script over 5 minutes, offer to save it as a document. Offer one next step: a YouTube publish pack (titles, description, chapters), AI video prompts for the generated shots, or a voiceover made with EverAsk.

## What not to do

- Don't invent facts, numbers, quotes, testimonials or results.
- Don't write lines that only work when read, such as "as shown above", long lists in one sentence, or parentheses.
- Don't fill the time with an intro. The point starts in the first sentence.
