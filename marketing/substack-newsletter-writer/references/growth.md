# Growth, Notes and paid tiers

Read this only when the user asks about growth, Notes strategy, recommendations or going paid. These notes are working guidance, not measured facts about the user's publication. Do not quote numbers from this file as data. If the user wants current figures (conversion rates, fees, how the feed ranks things), run one or two `web_search` calls, link what you found, and say what is not confirmed.

## How Substack surfaces writing

- Substack earns when readers subscribe to paid publications, so its feeds aim at subscriptions more than raw clicks. Outrage and engagement bait do not help; content good enough that a reader wants more does.
- Matching leans on audience overlap: readers of larger publications close to yours may see your work.
- Steady publishing on a consistent theme, with pieces that build on one another, tends to beat one-off hits.

## Notes

- Likes are widely reported as the main ranking signal, with restacks and replies after that. Engaging with other writers in your niche helps the algorithm learn who your readers are.
- Works: a genuine question, one surprising specific observation, a short take on something in your field, behind-the-scenes of your writing.
- Does not work: "check out my latest post", thread-style content copied from X, vague motivation.
- Use Notes as a cheap test: post a few ideas a week and write the long version of the ones that get a response.

## Recommendations

Writers choose by hand whom to recommend, and recommendations appear when a reader subscribes. To earn them: write well in a related niche, engage with the other writer's work over time, and suggest a swap only when the audiences truly overlap. Irrelevant swaps annoy new subscribers.

## Growth levers, rough order

1. Recommendations from related publications and word of mouth.
2. Consistent, high-quality publishing.
3. Notes as a testing pipeline, and guest posts or cross-references with complementary writers.
4. A clear homepage, a good welcome email and a pinned "Start here" post.
5. External traffic (LinkedIn, X, podcasts, search). Web posts gather search traffic over months; emails are read once.

## Free versus paid

- Free issues should be the best work: they drive growth and recommendations.
- Paid issues should add depth, access or exclusivity, not just a paywall on ordinary content.
- With a small list, keep everything free until there is an audience to convert. When the user wants a conversion or revenue estimate, do the arithmetic in `execute_code` from the user's own list size, price and conversion assumption, and label the conversion rate as their assumption.
- Other income that may beat small paid tiers: consulting, courses or products sold through the newsletter, sponsorships once the list is large.
- Writers who write about writing on Substack have a built-in audience; their growth numbers may not transfer to other niches.
