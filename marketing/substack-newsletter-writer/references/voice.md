# Voice matching

Voice matching is a method, not a feel. Use it when the user gives samples, a transcript, or asks you to ghostwrite.

## Process

1. **Rank the samples.** Transcripts and voice notes show how the person really talks. Social posts and chat messages come next, then published posts, then formal writing (the most edited, the least telling). Use at most the first 3 to 5 samples.
2. **Mark patterns.** Read for the categories below. A pattern that shows up in two or more samples is reliable. A pattern from one sample may belong to that context: note it as tentative.
3. **Write the guide** (6 to 10 markers). Each marker needs a short quoted fragment from the samples as proof. No quote, no marker.
4. **Draft against it.** Check each paragraph: does it use their words, their sentence length, their kind of opening and closing? Fix drift toward your own defaults.

## What to look for

- **Vocabulary**: their default words ("big", "huge", "significant"), filler words, technical depth, and words they avoid.
- **Sentences**: typical length (count words in 10 sentences using `execute_code`), fragments, questions, complexity.
- **Rhythm**: paragraph length, mix of short and long, prose or lists, emphasis style (bold, italics, caps).
- **Tone**: formal or casual, kind of humour or none, hedged or declarative, emotional range. Missing traits count: no emoji and no exclamation marks are markers too.
- **Metaphors**: where their comparisons come from (sport, kitchen, engineering) and whether they repeat.
- **Structure**: how they open (story, claim, question), how they close, how they use headers and transitions.

## Guide format (put it in the reply when you used samples)

```
Voice guide
- Register: <casual / professional / mixed>
- Typical sentence length: <n> words (from <k> sampled sentences)
- Humour and confidence: <style>
- Opens with: <pattern> ("<quote>")
- Closes with: <pattern> ("<quote>")
- Words they use: <list from samples>
- Words and moves they never use: <list>
- Signature move: <pattern> ("<quote>")
```

## Pitfalls

- Writing in your own voice after a few paragraphs. Re-check against the guide.
- Over-polishing a rough, direct voice. If they write in fragments, keep the fragments.
- Confusing formality with voice. Do not make an informal writer sound "professional".
- Anchoring on one sample. A talk and a newsletter can differ.
- Adding what they never do. One exclamation mark from a writer who uses none breaks the voice.
- Copying a sample's facts into the new piece. Take the style, not the content.

If the samples are too thin to support a marker (one short post), say so and keep the guide short.
