# Format rules: newsletter issue versus web post

Substack limits and features change. Treat the numbers here as working guidance and tell the user to check Substack's help pages for anything they depend on.

## Newsletter issue (email-first)

- **Subject line**: 60 characters or fewer; only about 35 to 40 show on a phone, so put the interesting part first. Specific beats vague. Promise something the issue delivers. No fake urgency.
- **Preview text**: about 90 characters, the first line of the body or the subtitle. It adds to the subject line and never repeats it.
- **Paragraphs**: 2 to 3 sentences. Email columns are narrow and long paragraphs become walls.
- **Subheadings**: one every 300 to 400 words, H2 and H3 only. Bold 1 to 2 key phrases per section so a skimmer still gets the argument.
- **Links**: descriptive anchor text, never "click here". Put the important link early. No URL shorteners.
- **Lists**: only for steps, quick comparisons or roundups. Not every section.
- **Dividers**: at most 2 or 3.
- **Length**: add a TL;DR at the top above about 1,500 words. Gmail clips very long emails (around 100 KB), so keep issues well under about 3,000 words.
- **Code**: inline for fewer than 5 lines, a fenced block up to about 10 lines, and for anything longer link to a Gist or repo with a 2 to 3 line excerpt.
- **Images**: a cover image and at most 1 or 2 inline. Many email clients block images, so never put key information only in an image. Always give alt text.
- **Buttons**: 3 to 5 words, one or two per issue. Embeds, polls and footnotes degrade in email (a link or preview), so do not rely on them.

## Web post (web-first)

- **Title**: under 60 characters, with the main keyword in the first 3 to 4 words. A punchy title for the feed is fine if the SEO title carries the keyword.
- **Subtitle**: under 10 words, written for people.
- **SEO title**: under 60 characters. **SEO description**: 150 to 160 characters, with a hook and the main keyword (otherwise Substack falls back to the subtitle, which is usually too short).
- **URL slug**: 3 to 6 words, hyphenated, main keyword in, stop words out, no dates.
- **Paragraphs**: 3 to 4 sentences. **Subheadings**: every 200 to 400 words, written as short summaries so the headings alone give the argument. H4 is usable.
- **Table of contents**: for posts above about 2,000 words, a bulleted list at the top.
- **Code**: up to 30 to 40 lines with language hints. Longer goes to a link.
- **Links**: 2 to 4 internal links to the writer's earlier posts, only if the user gave them. Never invent a link.
- **Media**: images, diagrams and embeds render reliably, so use them more freely. Alt text matters for accessibility and search.
- **Evergreen versus timely**: tutorials, frameworks and deep dives suit web posts (they gather search traffic over months). Commentary and announcements suit email issues. Some pieces can be both.
- **Sections**: group posts into 2 to 4 sections at most, if the user has a publication with many posts.

## Substack blocks worth suggesting

Suggest only where they help, and name them as suggestions in the issue (for example `[Subscribe button here]`).

- Subscribe button after the issue has delivered clear value. Share button after the strongest insight or at the end.
- Pull quote for the single most quotable line.
- Poll to draw replies (one per post at most; in email it is a link to vote on the web).
- Paywall divider only for paid posts: enough free value above it to hook, depth below it.
- Footnotes for tangents (reliable on web, less so in email).

## Notes (short posts inside Substack)

Notes have no strict length limit, but short ones do best: 2 to 5 sentences. A Note must stand alone and be worth reading without the link. Good Notes ask a real question, share one specific observation, or show part of the writing process. Weak Notes are "new post is out" with a link, tweet-style threads, or vague motivation. Never include hashtags.

## Adapting a post, talk or thread into an issue

- Cut a third to a half for email.
- Put the point in the first two sentences. Slow builds lose email readers.
- Add the personal layer (why the writer is sharing it) only from what the user gave.
- End with a specific ask, preferably a question readers can answer by replying.

## Image brief format

For each image idea give: placement ("cover", "after the hook", "between section 2 and 3"), purpose (set tone, break up text, illustrate a concept), and what it should show. Suggested aspect ratio: wide (about 16:9) for covers, about 3:2 for inline images.
