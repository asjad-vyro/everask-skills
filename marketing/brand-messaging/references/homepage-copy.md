# Homepage copy

Write the actual words for each section below, top to bottom, from the brand script. Keep every section short. Where a section needs proof the user didn't give (testimonials, logos, numbers, years), write a bracketed slot and list it under "Fill in before publishing". Never write a made-up quote or figure to fill the space.

## Output shape

For each section give a bold label, then the text a visitor would read, one labelled line per element:

```
**Top of page**
Headline: ...
Subhead: ...
Button: ...
```

Add one short italic line for a visual only where it helps (*Image: a freelancer closing a laptop on a Friday afternoon*). The copy is the deliverable, not a design brief.

## 1. Top of page (above the fold)
The most important space. A visitor should grasp the offer in about five seconds.
- **Headline:** what the customer wants, not what the business does. Eight words or fewer where you can. Give a main headline and two alternates. Shape of a good one: "Get Your Saturdays Back" (lawn care), "Meals Your Family Will Actually Eat" (meal delivery).
- **Subhead:** one sentence on how you help them get it.
- **Button:** the direct call to action, in action words. Repeat it in the navigation bar.
- **Image idea:** the customer in the success state, not the office or the team.

## 2. Stakes
Straight below the top of the page. Two or three lines on what staying stuck costs ("Don't let another year go by feeling..."). A taste, not a scare campaign.

## 3. Benefits
What is in it for them: three or four outcomes, each a short title plus one or two sentences. Outcomes, not features. Write them as "you" statements, based on what the user said the product does. Don't add features, speeds or promises they didn't state: a flat price is a price, not "no surprise invoices".

## 4. Guide
Empathy first, then authority.
- An empathy line: "We know what it's like to..."
- Authority: the proof the user gave (years, results, clients, credentials, press logos). If they gave none, write slots such as `[years in business]` and `[client logos or a real result]`.
- Don't tell the origin story: that makes the business the hero. Never write vetting, safety checks, ratings, credentials or a founder backstory the user didn't give.

## 5. Plan
A "How it works" block: exactly 3 steps (4 at most), each a number, a title of two or three words and one line. Finish with the direct call to action button. If the user stated agreement-plan commitments (cancel anytime, flat price, a guarantee), list them under the steps.

## 6. Explanation (only if the product is complex)
One or two lines pointing to a demo, a video or a screenshot walkthrough, with a transitional call to action ("See how it works"). Skip it for simple offers.

## 7. Testimonials
Header such as "What customers say". Use only real quotes the user supplied. Otherwise write two slots, `[Quote from a real customer about the result they got]` and `[Name, role or company]`, and add what to ask customers for: their situation before, what was hardest, and the result.

## 8. Lead magnet (transitional call to action)
For visitors who aren't ready to buy: "Not ready yet? Get [the free resource]". Use the resource the user mentioned. If there is none, suggest one (a checklist, a template, a short guide, a quiz) and mark it "suggested". The button must say what it really is.

## 9. Final call to action
A short "Ready to [outcome]?" line, with the direct call to action button and a one-line picture of success.

## 10. Footer
Contact details, standard links, and one more direct call to action button. Keep it simple and don't hide important links.

## Complete-page checklist
- A headline that states the customer's desire.
- One obvious call to action above the fold, repeated at least three times down the page.
- Stakes mentioned briefly.
- Three or four outcome-focused benefits.
- Empathy and authority both present.
- A 3-step plan.
- Testimonial slot or real quotes.
- A transitional offer.
- No "we" opening a headline, no jargon, no unproven superlatives.

## Other pages

Use the same logic on interior pages: customer first, one call to action, at least two on a long page.

- **Product or service page:** title naming the outcome; who it's for ("This is for [customer] who struggle with [problem]"); what's included; how it works (3 steps); testimonials about this service; pricing or packages; FAQ; final call to action.
- **About page:** headline on what the customer gets, not the company history; the mission as the philosophical problem ("We believe [customers] shouldn't have to..."); the team as guides, with bios about how they help; credentials (real ones); call to action.
- **Case study page:** a result headline ("How [client] got [result]"); the challenge (external and internal problem); the solution (the plan in action); the results, with figures only if they are real and approved; a client quote; call to action.
- **Pricing page:** a headline on choosing the right plan; a plan comparison with one clearly recommended option; the guarantee or risk reversal, only if real; pricing FAQ; a testimonial about value.
