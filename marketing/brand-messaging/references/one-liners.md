# One-liners

A one-liner is a single sentence that explains what the business does. It passes the "cocktail party test": someone can repeat it after hearing it once. Keep it to 25 words or fewer.

## The formula

```
We help [CUSTOMER] who [PROBLEM] [SOLUTION] so they can [RESULT].
```

| Part | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Customer | Who you serve | "busy professionals" |
| Problem | Their struggle | "who don't have time to cook" |
| Solution | What you provide | "get chef-prepared meals delivered" |
| Result | The transformation | "eat well without the hassle" |

## Examples by industry

Use these for shape only. Write the user's own, from their own words.

**B2B software**
- Project management: "We help remote teams who struggle with scattered communication stay aligned so they can ship faster without endless meetings."
- Accounting: "We help small business owners who dread tax season automate their bookkeeping so they can stop worrying and focus on growth."

**Professional services**
- Financial advisor: "We help families who feel confused about retirement build a clear plan so they can stop worrying and start living."
- Consulting: "We help CEOs who are stuck at a growth plateau find the bottleneck so they can break through to the next level."

**E-commerce**
- Meal delivery: "We help busy parents who never find time to cook get fresh ingredients delivered weekly so dinner feels easy."
- Skincare: "We help people with sensitive skin find products that actually work so they can feel confident without irritation."

**Local services**
- Home cleaning: "We help busy homeowners who never have time to clean get their weekends back so they can focus on family, not chores."
- Landscaping: "We help homeowners who are embarrassed by their yard transform it so they can be proud to have guests over."

**Coaching**
- Business coach: "We help entrepreneurs who work too many hours build systems that run without them so they can get their life back."
- Leadership coach: "We help new managers who feel unprepared develop executive presence so they can lead with confidence."

## Alternative forms

Use these for the two alternates. Each alternate also stays at 25 words or fewer, so tighten a form that runs long.

**Problem, solution, result**
`[PROBLEM STATEMENT]. We [SOLUTION]. So you can [RESULT].`
"Hiring is broken. We screen candidates and surface the best fits. So you can hire faster without missing great talent."

**Before, after, bridge**
`Before: [CURRENT PAIN]. After: [DESIRED STATE]. Bridge: [YOUR SOLUTION].`
"Before: drowning in support tickets. After: instant answers that delight customers. Bridge: a chatbot that handles the repeat questions for you."

**"We exist because"**
`[TARGET] shouldn't have to [PAIN]. That's why we [SOLUTION].`
"Small businesses shouldn't have to choose between powerful software and affordable pricing. That's why we built both into one tool."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it fails | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Too vague | Means nothing | "We help companies succeed" |
| Too long | Can't be remembered | 50-word explanation |
| No problem | No hook | "We provide consulting services" |
| Feature-focused | Not compelling | "We offer cloud-based project management" |
| No transformation | No desire | "We do digital marketing" |
| Jargon | Confuses people | "We leverage synergies to optimize ROI" |
| Several audiences | Dilutes impact | "We help everyone with everything" |

## Tests

1. **Cocktail party test:** tell it to someone and walk away. Could they tell someone else what you do?
2. **"So what?" test:** does it make someone want to learn more? If the reaction is "okay, cool", it is too weak.
3. **Specificity test:** could a competitor use the same sentence? If yes, it is too generic.
4. **Benefit test:** is the result something people actually want? "Save time" is weak; "Get your weekends back" is strong.

## Where to use it

Website header, email signature, social bios, LinkedIn headline, the opening of sales calls, podcast and event introductions, and team training (everyone should be able to say it).
