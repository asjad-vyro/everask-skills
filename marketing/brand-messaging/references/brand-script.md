# Brand script template

Fill every line from what the user told you, in their words where you can. Mark anything you assumed with "(assumed)". Where the user gave no proof, leave a bracketed slot instead of inventing it.

In the reply, show each part as a numbered item with one line per element, like this:

```
1. Customer: [who] | Wants: [one outcome] | Wants to become: [identity]
2. Problem: Villain: ... | External: ... | Internal: ... | Philosophical: ...
3. Guide: Empathy: ... | Authority: ...
4. Plan: Process: 1. ... 2. ... 3. ... | Agreement: ...
5. Call to action: Direct: ... | Transitional: ...
6. Success: ...
7. Failure: ...
```

## 1. Customer (the hero)
- **Who:** the specific role or situation, not "everyone".
- **Wants:** ONE specific desire, stated as an outcome in the customer's words. Several desires dilute the story. Tie it to something real: time, money, relationships, peace of mind, standing.
- **Wants to become:** the identity they are after ("the person who has it under control").

## 2. Problem
- **Villain:** the specific, relatable source of the problem: a situation, process or habit ("the shoebox of receipts", "jargon built to confuse you"). Not a named person and not a vague word like "complexity".
- **External:** the tangible problem the business solves.
- **Internal:** how it makes them feel (overwhelmed, embarrassed, behind, in the dark). This is what drives the purchase, so never skip it.
- **Philosophical:** why it is wrong ("Nobody should need a finance degree to...").

## 3. Guide (the business)
- **Empathy:** one line showing you understand the struggle ("We know what it's like to...").
- **Authority:** up to three proof points the user gave (results, years, clients served, credentials, press, real testimonials). If none were given, write slots such as `[years in business]`.
- Do not make the origin story the centrepiece: that makes the business the hero again.

## 4. Plan
- **Process plan:** 3 steps (4 at most), each a short action name and one line. Step 1 is small and easy ("Book a call"). More than 4 steps overwhelms.
- **Agreement plan:** commitments that remove risk (cancel anytime, flat price, a guarantee). Only those the user stated; label any others "suggested, confirm it's true".

## 5. Call to action
- **Direct:** the main ask in action words ("Book a free 15-minute call"). One per page.
- **Transitional:** a lower-commitment ask for people who aren't ready ("Get the checklist", "Take the quiz", "Watch the demo"). If the user has no such offer, suggest one and mark it "suggested".
- The label must match what happens on click. Never call a paid action "free".

## 6. Success
- **Status:** how others will see them.
- **Completeness:** what they will finally have.
- **Self-realization:** who they become.
- Make it concrete ("Open your books in April and know exactly where you stand"). Use figures only if the user gave them.

## 7. Failure
- What staying stuck costs them: wasted time, money, stress, another year of the same. A taste, not a threat. Pair it with the success picture for contrast.

## Check before you use it
- The customer wants something specific, not something vague.
- The internal problem is named (feelings, not only facts).
- Empathy and authority both show.
- The plan is 3 to 4 steps.
- The direct call to action is clear and specific.
- Success is a picture the customer wants; failure creates stakes without manipulating.
- The one-liner can be repeated after hearing it once.
- Nothing in it is a fact the user didn't give you.
