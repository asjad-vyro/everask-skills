# Email sequences

Nurture emails that move a subscriber through the brand script toward the direct call to action. Every email should give value, position the business as the guide, and move the reader toward the outcome they want.

Rules:
- These are shapes, not facts. Fill them only with what the user gave you. Where an email needs a case study, quote, result or number the user didn't supply, write a bracketed slot and list it under "Fill in before sending". Never invent an anecdote.
- Everything is a draft for the user to review. Don't send anything.
- One direct call to action per email.

## Welcome sequence (5 emails)

It starts when someone joins the list. New subscribers are usually at their most engaged, so use the first week well.

**Email 1, Day 0: deliver and welcome.** Subjects: "Your [lead magnet] is here", "Here's your free [guide]" (only if it is free).
Body: hello; the promised resource with its link; one tip on where to start; what to expect (how often, what kind of value, unsubscribe anytime); a P.S. inviting replies.

**Email 2, Day 2: problem and empathy.** Subjects: "The [problem] no one talks about", "Why [common approach] doesn't work".
Body: name the wall most people in their position hit; the common mistake and where it leads; one line of real empathy; the deeper issue under the surface problem; one quick action they can take; a teaser for the next email.

**Email 3, Day 4: authority and proof.** Subjects: "How [client] got [result]" (only with a real case), "The method behind [result]".
Body: a real case in four beats (their problem, what they tried, what changed, the result), then the process in three steps. With no real case, lead with the method and the credentials the user gave, using slots for anything missing.

**Email 4, Day 6: the plan.** Subjects: "Your 3-step path to [outcome]", "The simple plan that works".
Body: from where they are to where they want to be in three steps, each a name plus one or two lines. No 47-step process. Offer a resource for step 1 as the transitional call to action. Tease the next email.

**Email 5, Day 8: direct call to action.** Subjects: "Ready to [outcome]?", "Your next step".
Body: a three-bullet recap of the week; two choices (do it alone with the roadmap you gave them, or let the business help); the direct call to action; what happens when they act, with a risk reversal only if the user offers one; one light line on what waiting costs; a P.S. deadline only if it is genuine.

## Ongoing nurture

Send three value emails for every sales email.

- **Value emails:** quick win (one actionable tip and why it works); story and lesson (hook, what happened, the insight, how to apply it); common mistake ("Stop [mistake]", why it fails, the better approach); FAQ answer (the question, a short answer, a longer one, an example).
- **Sales emails:** problem agitation ("Still [struggling with problem]?", the solution, how it works, the button); customer story (a real customer, a real result, a real quote); deadline (only if genuine: what ends, when, what changes after).

## Subject lines

Common formats: number plus promise; question; "how to [outcome] without [pain]"; curiosity gap; personalisation; specificity; story hint; direct benefit. Keep them short (around 40 to 50 characters is commonly advised), avoid spam triggers (all caps, "FREE!!!", "$$$"), and use the preview text.

## Segments

- By behaviour: opened but didn't click (resend with a new angle); clicked but didn't buy (case study, answer objections); bought (cross-sell); inactive for 30 or more days (re-engagement); very engaged (early access).
- By source: blog subscribers (educational); webinar attendees (application and offers); lead magnet downloaders (that topic); trial users (onboarding and activation).

## Re-engagement (3 emails)

For people who have stopped opening.
1. **"Is [topic] still relevant to you?"** Two buttons: keep me subscribed, or unsubscribe me.
2. **Best content recap:** the three emails that got the most replies, one line each.
3. **Last email:** say you'll stop emailing if you don't hear back. Only promise removal if the user will actually do it.

## What to watch

Open rate (subject line and sender trust), click rate (relevance and call to action), unsubscribes (content or frequency mismatch), replies (depth of engagement). Use the user's own past numbers as the benchmark. Falling opens point to subject lines or fatigue; clicks without conversions point to a mismatch between the offer and the landing page.
