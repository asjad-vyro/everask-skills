# Sales conversations

The salesperson is the guide, not the hero. The old way is "let me tell you about our company, our features, our awards". The better way is "tell me about your challenge; here's how we help people like you succeed".

Rules:
- Examples show shape. Use only results, customer counts and prices the user gave you. Where one is missing, write a bracketed slot and list it under "Fill in before you use it". Never invent a statistic for the call.
- Anything about the prospect's costs comes from the prospect, in their own numbers.

## Call structure

**1. Opening: establish the problem (about 5 minutes).**
"Before I tell you about us, I'd love to understand your situation. What prompted you to take this call today?"
Follow-ups: how long has this been a challenge; what have you tried; what happens if nothing changes?

**2. Exploration: understand the job (about 15 minutes).** Ask at all three levels.
- External: "What's the tangible problem? What does it cost you in time or money?"
- Internal: "How does this make you and your team feel? What's the most frustrating part?"
- Philosophical: "Why is fixing this important to you? What do you believe should be possible?"

**3. Position as the guide (about 5 minutes).**
- Empathy: "I hear this a lot. Watching good [leads/customers/hours] slip away is frustrating, especially after the work you put in."
- Authority: "We've helped [real number] [type of customer] with exactly this, and [real, documented result]." Skip any part you can't back up.

**4. The plan (about 10 minutes).** Three steps: "Here's how we typically help: first we [step 1]; then we [step 2]; finally we [step 3]. We handle most of the heavy lifting."

**5. Stakes (about 5 minutes).**
- Success: "Picture [timeframe] from now: [the problem is gone, described in their words]. How would that change things?"
- Failure, light touch: "And if you keep doing what you're doing, where will you be in a year?"

**6. Call to action (about 5 minutes).** Name one clear next step: "Based on what you've shared, I think we can help. The next step is a [length] demo where I show you how it would work for your team. Does [specific time] suit you?" If they hesitate: "Would it help if I sent a case study from a similar company while you think?" (only if one exists).

## Discovery questions

- **Their world:** "Walk me through how you handle [process] today." "What have you tried? Why didn't it work?" "What does this problem cost you?"
- **Their goals:** "What does success look like?" "When does this need to be solved by?" "Who else cares about solving it?" "How do you usually decide on something like this?"
- **Constraints:** "Is there budget set aside?" "Who would be involved in rolling it out?" "What systems does it need to work with?" "Any deadlines?"
- **Emotional probes:** "What frustrates you most?" "What worries you if this doesn't get solved?" "What would fixing it mean to you personally?"

## Objections: acknowledge, explore, reframe

Acknowledge ("That's a fair concern."), explore ("Tell me more about what's behind it."), then reframe.

| Objection | Explore | Reframe options |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|
| "It's too expensive." | "Too expensive compared to what?" | The cost of the problem today; what it would be worth if solved; other options or packages |
| "We need to think about it." | "What specifically do you need to think through?" | "Is there something I didn't cover?"; "What happens if you wait another quarter?"; offer materials to share |
| "We're looking at competitors." | "Smart. What matters most in your decision?" | How you differ on what they named; invite a side-by-side on their criteria; "What would make us the clear choice?" |
| "Now isn't the right time." | "What would make it the right time?" | "What happens if you wait six months?"; is there an event that changes timing; agree when to reconnect |
| "I need to talk to my boss or team." | "What do you think their main concerns will be?" | Ask what would help them make the case; offer to join a call; send a short summary to share |

## Presenting price

Frame value first. Don't open with the number. Start with the prospect's own cost of the problem, then the benefit, then the price: "You told me this costs you about [their figure] a month. If we [result], that's worth [their figure]. The investment is [price]." Offer a choice that makes yes easier (for example annual or monthly) only if the user actually offers both.

## Follow-up

- **After a first call, no decision.** Day 1: thank-you and summary (their challenge, its impact, how you help, the next step you proposed). Day 3: one useful resource or a real, similar case. Day 7: a short check-in offering another call.
- **After a demo.** Day 1: recap and materials. Day 3: address the likely objection. Day 7: gentle urgency, only if genuine. Day 14: move them to long-term nurture if there's no reply.

## Scripts for key moments

- **Cold call opener:** "Hi [Name], this is [you] from [company]. I know I'm calling out of the blue. Do you have 30 seconds? We help [customers] with [problem]. I noticed [real reason for calling]. Is that something you're dealing with?"
- **Asking for the meeting:** "Based on what you've shared, a 30-minute conversation would show whether we can help. Are you open to that?" If hesitant: "No obligation. How's [specific time]?"
- **Closing:** "It sounds like [solution] would solve [their problem] and help you [their outcome]. I'd recommend we start with [package or plan]. Should I send the agreement to review?"
- **"Send me information":** "Happy to. So I send the right thing, what would be most useful: pricing, case studies or technical details? And once you've reviewed it, could we have a quick call? How's [specific time]?"
