# Review Requests and Replies (Local Business)

A good result is text the owner can paste today: replies that sound like a real person, name the specific thing the reviewer mentioned, never argue, never promise or admit more than the facts support, and review requests that follow Google's rules. Finish the whole job in one reply from what the user gave. Do not ask a list of questions first; where something is missing, use a clearly marked `[placeholder]` or state an assumption and go on.

## Step 1: Work out the job

Pick what the user asked for. More than one can apply.

- **A. Reply to reviews they pasted** (go to Step 3).
- **B. Get more reviews**: request messages and a plan (Step 4).
- **C. A specific problem**: fake or abusive review, a review that names an employee, a legal threat, a review on another platform (Step 5).
- **D. A staff guide**: a one-page "how we answer reviews" for the team (use Steps 3 and 5, written as rules plus examples).

Note the business type, name, location, owner or manager name and phone if given. If the business is in healthcare, law, or another regulated field, read `references/industry-rules.md` before writing anything.

## Step 2: Facts rule (applies to everything)

- Use only facts the user gave or the review itself states. Never invent a service, a staff name, a policy, a refund, a phone number, an offer, a review count or a result.
- A reply may say what the business will do next ("please call [name] at [phone]") but must not claim what happened in the dispute unless the user told you. If a complaint is factual and the user did not say what really occurred, do not confirm or deny it: thank, apologise for the experience, and move it offline.
- Do not confirm any unverified detail of a complaint, including timing or lateness ("being three hours late is not our standard" confirms it). Do not use "make it right", "fix this", "not our standard" or "understand what went wrong" unless the user confirmed the fault.
- In positive replies, do not add claims about the business ("X is a big part of what we do") or embellish what the reviewer said ("cleaned up" stays "cleaned up", not "cleaner than he found it").
- Never admit legal liability or fault beyond facts the user confirmed. "We're sorry your visit fell short of what you expected" is fine; "we made a mistake and damaged your floor" is not unless the user said so.
- Never reveal private customer details (full name, address, order, appointment, health or legal matter) beyond what the reviewer wrote themselves, and in regulated fields not even that.
- Do not offer discounts, gifts, refunds or free service in a public reply. Resolution happens offline.
- Do not state ranking-factor claims or statistics as fact. If you mention that review recency and responses help local visibility, say it is widely believed and commonly cited, not guaranteed.

## Step 3: Write the replies

For every pasted review, give one reply, in the order pasted. Before each, write one line: the star rating if known and the main point of the review.

**Positive reviews (4-5 stars)**
1. Thank the reviewer by first name (or "Thanks for the kind words" if no name).
2. Mention the specific thing they praised (the service, the person, the result), in the reviewer's own terms, without adding or enlarging anything.
3. Work in the business type or town once, naturally. Do not stuff keywords.
4. Close with a short, warm line. 2-4 sentences. Vary the openings and endings across replies so they do not look copy-pasted.

**Mixed or negative reviews (1-3 stars)**
1. Thank them for the feedback.
2. Apologise for the experience, not for blame the user has not confirmed.
3. Address the specific point calmly in one sentence, without defending or arguing. If the complaint is about a fact the user has not confirmed, stay neutral.
4. Take it offline: a named person and a phone or email (use `[placeholder]` if not given).
5. 3-5 sentences, no sarcasm, no blame on the customer, no details of the transaction.
6. When the user has not confirmed the facts, use this safe shape: "Thank you for the feedback, [first name]. I'm sorry your experience didn't meet your expectations. I'd like to hear more; please call [name] at [phone]." Before writing a Notes claim that a reply "neither confirms nor denies", reread the reply and remove any sentence that accepts a complaint detail.

**Other cases**
- A question inside a review: answer it briefly and publicly if the answer is general.
- A star rating with no text: a short thank-you, no assumptions about what they liked.
- A review that mixes praise with a gripe: thank for the praise, then address the gripe.
- Review mentions an employee by name: thank or acknowledge the team; do not discuss staff discipline publicly.
- Suspected fake: reply politely and generically once ("We can't find a record of this visit...") only if the user says they have no record; otherwise follow Step 5.

Reply timing to include in the notes: positive within a day or two, negative as soon as possible, ideally the same day.

## Step 4: Review requests and plan

If the user asked for a request message, write the messages. If they asked how to get more reviews, write the messages and the plan.

**Messages to write** (each short, with `[Google review link]` and `[business name]` placeholders if not given):
1. An SMS (under about 300 characters).
2. An email with a subject line.
3. A verbal ask script for staff (two sentences).
4. Optionally a QR-card line.

**Rules for every request message (no exceptions)**
- Ask for an honest review. Never offer a discount, gift, entry, refund or any reward in exchange, and never say or imply a better review earns something. Offering rewards for reviews breaks Google's review policy.
- Do not review-gate: send the same ask to every customer, not only the happy ones. Do not write "if you were happy, leave a review; if not, contact us" as a filter.
- No "5 stars" wording. Ask for "an honest review" or "your feedback".
- If the user wants to offer a discount or perk, do not put it in or next to any review request. Do not write a combined message or a "separate thank-you" workaround in the same flow. Say any discount must be unrelated to reviews and sent separately, or leave it out.
- Verbal scripts and messages must not start with "If you were happy..." or any condition; use one neutral ask for every customer, e.g. "Would you mind leaving us an honest Google review? It really helps us."
- Do not state response-rate or timing statistics ("reply rates drop fast") as fact; say "often" or "commonly suggested".
- Do not ask employees or family to review, and do not use an in-office kiosk or shared device for many reviews.
- Do not ask for reviews on Yelp directly; Yelp discourages solicitation. A neutral "find us on Yelp" is not an ask.

**The Google link**: `https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=YOUR_PLACE_ID`. Tell the user to replace `YOUR_PLACE_ID` with their Place ID, or use the short review link from their Google Business Profile.

**Plan** (keep to one screen):
1. Best moment to ask: right after the job is done or a good interaction, ask in person first, then follow up by text within a day.
2. Send requests steadily (daily or weekly) rather than in occasional bursts; a steady flow looks natural.
3. A rough monthly goal tied to the size the user gave. If they gave no size, give a modest range as an assumption and label it as such.
4. Who replies to reviews and how fast.
5. Check reviews at least weekly across Google and the main industry platforms (see `references/industry-rules.md` for platform names by industry).
6. A 90-day target the user can adjust, labelled as an example.

## Step 5: Fake, abusive or risky reviews

Give a short numbered plan:
1. Do not reply in anger. Do not accuse the reviewer publicly.
2. Check your records: does a customer, job or visit match? Do this before saying anything.
3. If it breaks the platform's content rules (spam, not an actual experience, conflict of interest, harassment, off-topic), flag it in Google Business Profile (the three-dot menu on the review, then report) and keep screenshots and dates. Removal is not guaranteed and can take days or weeks.
4. Post one calm public reply. Draft it: say you cannot match the review to a customer record (only if true), invite them to contact you, and keep it short.
5. If the review is removed or stays, keep earning real reviews so one review matters less.
6. For a review that makes a defamatory or legal claim, suggest the user speak to a lawyer before any public reply. This is not legal advice.

## Output shape

Return, in this order, only the parts the user asked for:

1. `## Replies` : each review quoted in one short line, followed by the reply in a blockquote.
2. `## Review request messages` : the SMS, email, verbal script, each labelled.
3. `## Plan` : numbered steps from Step 4.
4. `## Fake or problem reviews` : if asked.
5. `## Notes` : one short list of assumptions made, placeholders to fill in, and anything the user should confirm before posting (for example a fact in a complaint you could not verify).

Everything is a draft for the user to review and post themselves; do not claim anything was posted. Mention once that platform review rules change, so check Google's current review policy before launching a request campaign.
