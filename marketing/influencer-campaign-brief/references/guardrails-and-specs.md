# Guardrails, freedom and deliverable specs

## Lock the facts, leave the voice

Lock (non-negotiables): discount codes and offer terms exactly as the deal defines them; mandatory legal wording, verbatim; prohibited claims and comparisons; disclosure wording and placement; product-name spelling and pronunciation; accuracy standard (no invented features, results or customer status).

Leave open (preferences): speaking style, pacing, setting, personal stories, how the product enters the story, the hook (suggest angles, never dictate wording), editing, cuts, music (within the licensing rule), caption phrasing outside the mandatory elements.

Never include: word-for-word scripts, camera angles, cut timing, music choice, dictated caption text, or the whole brand style guide. They take the voice the brand is paying for and remove no risk the locked list has not already removed. Do not coach an "honest opinion" framing around paid content.

## Freedom levels

| Level | Creator controls | Brief locks | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| High (default) | concept, setting, hook, script | messages, disclosure, prohibited claims | awareness goal, repeat creator, near live date, no in-house reviewer |
| Medium | adapts a given concept direction | concept direction plus all High locks | consideration goal, first collaboration, creator new to the category |
| Low | voice and delivery inside a framework | structure, offer, code, CTA plus all locks | metric is redemptions or signups; regulated category needing script approval |

Never grant freedom on paper and take it back at review: judge drafts against the non-negotiables only. Low needs review rounds to enforce its framework, so rule it out when the deal has one round or fewer.

## Which guardrails to spend the 5 to 10 rules on

In order: (1) verbatim legal wording and disclosure; (2) prohibited claims, each with approved alternative wording; (3) accuracy standards; (4) competitor mentions, when comparisons are likely; (5) brand-safety adjacency, if placement risk is real; (6) sensitive topics, each named explicitly. Add the lower ones only when the brand's situation calls for them.

Regulated categories (health, fitness, beauty, supplements, finance, security, HR): include a "claims you cannot make" list with approved alternatives, flag before-and-after content for compliance review, and name the approver in the review section. State that legal should check the claims before the brief is sent.

## Deliverable fields

For each deliverable fill: platform or channel type; format; quantity; length (seconds for video and audio, word count for written); aspect ratio (visual formats); caption, hashtag and @mention requirements; link destination and mechanism; promo code and where it must be visible (spoken, on screen, caption); audio licensing constraint; draft deadline; posting window; disclosure placement for that format; raw-file delivery if the deal includes it. Write "creator's choice" where a field is deliberately open; silence reads as an oversight.

Commonly used specs (platform specs change: tell the user to check current platform documentation, and never present these as the deal's terms): story-format 9:16, with disclosure on every frame that carries promotion, not only the first; short-form video 9:16, disclosure spoken and on screen; static in-feed 1:1 or 4:5, disclosure in the first caption line.

B2B formats: sponsored newsletter slot (word count, position in the issue, number of links, subject-line mention yes or no, disclosure line); host-read podcast segment (duration, pre, mid or post-roll, talking-point count, code read aloud, disclosure spoken at the start); long-form professional-network post (length, first-line disclosure, link in post or first comment). In B2B, the credibility layer is the creator's hands-on experience; business metrics such as demo requests measure it afterwards.

## Paid amplification and audio

If the content could ever run as paid media, say so in the brief before the creator picks music: trending platform audio is generally not licensed for paid use, and a late declaration forces a re-edit. In thought-leader ad formats the ad is the creator's own post and edits must come from them, so settle final wording before the organic post goes live. Whitelisting, usage window and exclusivity are deal terms: point to the agreement, never set them.

## Review rounds and timeline

The round count in the brief equals the deal's count. Never invent it and never impose a typical number. Suggested schedule when the user gave no dates: brief sent, creator questions within 2 business days, concept approval, first draft, one revision window per round included, posting window, disclosure check after posting, analytics after the reporting window. Regulated or B2B chains with legal or product review need an extra dated review step; do not shorten the creator's drafting time to fit it.
