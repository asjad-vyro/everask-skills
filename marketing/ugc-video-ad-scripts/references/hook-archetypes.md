# Hook archetypes

Pick 3 to 5 distinct archetypes per concept. Example lines are illustrations only: replace every noun and number with wording from the user's brief or evidence.

## The triad

Every hook is three channels: the spoken line (opens the loop), on-screen text (anchors the claim for sound-off viewers), the opening visual (stops the scroll). They must carry different information. The strongest hooks fire two channels at once, for example direct address over a pattern-interrupt visual. On-screen text: 3 to 7 words. Talent to camera generally beats voiceover over B-roll, and a real screen recording of the product helps.

## Tier 1: dialogue-only, cheapest and strongest framing. Start here.

1. Skeptic's turn: open with doubt, pivot to the product. "I thought this was another [thing nobody uses]."
2. Problem callout: name the exact felt struggle. "If you [specific annoying thing], stop." Generic pain is invisible; specificity is the mechanic.
3. POV or relatability: a hyper-specific situation. "POV: it's [specific moment] and [specific problem]."

## Tier 2: dialogue-only, framing only

4. Question: the question the buyer actually types, in their words.
5. First-person confession: "I was doing [common thing] completely wrong." Needs lived-in detail or it reads fake.
6. Curiosity gap: withhold the noun. Must pay off in the body.
7. Reverse psychology: "Don't buy this if you [trait]."
8. Ironic list: "3 reasons you should NOT try this."
9. Contrarian: "Everyone says X. That advice is why Y keeps happening." The body must defend the claim.

## Tier 3: asset-gated. Strongest proof, but only when the asset exists and is cleared.

10. Proof-first: lead with a result, number or demo money-shot the user actually owns and may show.
11. Silent review: no spoken line; the product does something on camera, text carries the claim. Needs footage or a screen recording.
12. Bold claim: specific and falsifiable, and the advertiser can substantiate it.
13. Before/after contrast: two states in the first beat. Needs two setups and a typicality line; restricted outright in health and weight-loss.

Promote tier 3 when the asset already exists and is cleared, when the user wants a reusable asset library, or after tier-1 hooks have all performed about the same.

## Ruled out means deleted

- Regulated category barring comparison: delete before/after.
- No product footage or screen recording possible: delete silent review.
- No documented result cleared for use: delete proof-first and bold claim.
Name each deletion with its reason in the package.

## Quality bar per hook

- Generic test: if a direct competitor could say it word for word, rewrite.
- Hit at least two of: Unique, Urgent, Ultra-specific, Useful.
- Spoken line lands in about 2 seconds aloud.
- Works muted: visual plus text alone communicate the premise.
- A concrete noun beats an adjective. Withholding beats announcing.
- Promise only what the body delivers.

## Opening-frame anti-patterns

- A static frame or logo card first.
- A slow build to the product.
- Footage that contradicts the line (calm claim over frantic B-roll).

## B2B skew

Lead with problem callout, question, then proof-first (only with a cleared customer result), then contrarian, all framed as teaching, not pitching. Credential the speaker in the hook ("I run [role] at [type of company]") using only facts the user gave. Avoid discount-urgency and haul openings.
