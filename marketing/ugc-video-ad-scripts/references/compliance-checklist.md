# Compliance checklist

Rules that change the words on the page. This is not legal advice. Regulated categories (health, finance, supplements, legal services) need the advertiser's own legal review before anything is filmed. Rules change: before citing a specific law, section or penalty in a reply, check it with `web_search` and link the source; otherwise describe the requirement in general terms.

## Disclose material connections

Disclose whenever the performer received anything: payment, free or discounted product, affiliate commission, employment, a family relationship.

- Put the disclosure in the ad itself, clear and hard to miss, spoken or on-screen. A caption or bio mention is not enough.
- The platform's paid-partnership label may not be enough alone. Use it and also write a disclosure into the script.
- Plain and early: spoken "they sent it to me to try", on-screen "Paid partnership with [brand]". Vague credit ("thanks to [brand]") does not name the relationship.
- Employee performers: "I work at [brand] and here's how we actually use it."
- Reposting a creator's organic content as a paid ad does not remove the need to disclose.

## Markets

Ask or assume which markets the ad runs in, and write the disclosure in each market's language. Rules stack: an ad running in two regions must satisfy both. In general, the US requires clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections and bans fake or undisclosed-incentive reviews; the EU treats hidden advertising as misleading; France requires a specific French-language label for commercial collaborations. Verify current wording before relying on it.

## Actors and personas

- A paid actor presented as a customer must be disclosed as not an actual consumer, in the ad.
- Never script a fabricated testimonial or an invented "real user" story. Composite stories must be framed as such.

## Results and typicality

- A testimonial highlighting a benefit reads as a typical result. If the result is not typical, script the generally expected result; a bare "results not typical" is not enough.
- Number claims and before/after contrasts must be honest and substantiated. Use only numbers the user supplied.

## Claims and regulated categories

- A testimonial is not evidence. Health, efficacy and finance claims need substantiation the advertiser holds; otherwise soften or cut.
- Do not assert or imply the viewer's health, financial status or other protected attributes. Rewrite second-person "Do you have [condition]?" phrasing out of health and finance scripts.
- Specific numeric health testimonials (a lab value moving from one number to another) are routinely rejected: cut them.
- Before/after and body-focused imagery is restricted in health and weight-loss categories.
- Fake urgency is a deceptive claim: use a real deadline or none.

## Script-pass checklist (every script, every item)

1. Markets named and a disclosure written for each, in each language.
2. Material connection: disclosure in an early beat plus the platform label.
3. Performer not a real user: non-consumer disclosure in the ad.
4. Every quote traceable to real evidence, else cut or marked as example phrasing.
5. Result stated: typical, or a generally-expected-result line added.
6. Health, efficacy or financial claim: substantiation confirmed with the user or the claim is rewritten out.
7. Regulated category: whole package flagged for the advertiser's own legal review.
8. Usage rights for the creator's footage to be signed before filming.
