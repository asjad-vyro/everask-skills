# Script examples

Illustration of the output shape only. The product, quotes and sources here are made up: in real use every quote comes from the user's evidence, or the package is labelled UNVALIDATED. Never copy these lines.

## Worked B2C package (shape)

**Concept**: parents of school-age kids who cook on weeknights. Motivation: "[verbatim customer quote]" (source). Format: creator to camera in their kitchen, phone on the counter. Offer: [free trial length the user gave]. Duration: 20 seconds, about 50 spoken words. Validation: grounded or UNVALIDATED.

**Hook variants** (body stays fixed; test these against it)

| # | Archetype | Spoken line | On-screen text | Opening visual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem callout | "It's 5:30 and I still don't know what we're eating." | decision fatigue is real | Creator staring into an open fridge |
| 2 | First-person confession | "I used to plan meals in the pickup line. Badly." | my old system: panic | Phone screen full of screenshotted recipes |
| 3 | Question | "What's actually for dinner tonight?" | the question I stopped dreading | Creator mid-chop looks up at camera |

**Body** (example phrasing, not a locked read)

| Time | Spoken | On-screen text | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-7 s | "The deciding was the exhausting part, not the cooking." | deciding > cooking | Creator talks, hand on hip |
| 7-13 s | "It builds the week around what my kids will actually eat and writes the list itself." | filter, then list done | Screen recording of the app |
| 13-16 s | "Sunday I swap what I don't like. Five minutes." | [time the user confirms] | Creator swipes two meals away |

**CTA** (locked): spoken "Try one Sunday with it and see." On-screen: `[offer] · link below`.

**Delivery notes**: talk like venting to a friend, mildly amused, not salesy. Real kitchen, daylight, clutter is fine. Hold the phone up at beat 3. Cut every 2 to 4 seconds. First frame is the hook visual. Do: keep small imperfections. Don't: read it like a presenter.

**Compliance**: creator received free access, so spoken "they gave me the app to try" in beat 1, plus the platform paid-partnership label.

## Negative example: what not to ship

> Hook (spoken and on-screen identical): "Discover the revolutionary meal-planning solution busy families love!"
> Body: "MealApp is the #1 app with cutting-edge AI. Thousands of happy customers agree. Sarah from Ohio says it cured her stress completely."
> CTA: "Download now! Follow us! Use code SAVE20 today only! Link in bio!"

Wrong: brand voice in a person's mouth; spoken and on-screen text duplicated and no opening visual; hook promises nothing specific; invented testimonial ("Sarah from Ohio"); unsubstantiated cure claim; four stacked CTAs; fake urgency; no disclosure; no delivery notes.
